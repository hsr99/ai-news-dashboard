Anthropic's threat report: autonomous exploit foundries and AI supply-chain attacks are here

SafetyWeb

Anthropic says a majority of the cyber operations it disrupted now used AI for direct execution or orchestration, not just advice. A Chinese-speaking group ran parallel agent swarms with persistent campaign memory and a continuous binary-reversing/exploit loop; other attackers targeted AI evaluation sandboxes, LiteLLM deployments, and wrappers to steal production API keys.

Why it matters: secure the whole agent system, not only the model prompt. External inputs need hostile-data treatment; tool workers need isolation and bounded authority; API keys and sessions need the same controls as production credentials.

Two silent AutoRound mistakes - and why your calibration corpus is part of the quantizer

CraftWeb

The Archsloth team had optimized with AutoRound's W4A16 scheme, then exported Q4_K_M, so the exporter quietly rebuilt a different quantization and discarded most of the search. Matching the scheme and enabling SignRoundV2 cut KL divergence from the bf16 teacher by 21-54% across ten language/code axes at the same file size; calibration language and sample interleaving materially changed results.

Why it matters: a valid artifact and falling loss curve do not prove the quantization path you intended actually shipped. Match search and export schemes, test monotonicity, measure distribution drift rather than perplexity alone, and version the calibration corpus like code.

Iris-mini and Iris-pro: open-weight search agents expose the context-management tax

Open modelsAgentsWeb

AllSpark released 35B and 397B search agents, plus an OpenAI-compatible harness, tools, context strategies, and four evals. The useful result is architectural: context management lifted Iris-mini by as much as 21.2 BrowseComp points, and a failed first attempt compressed into a note improved the retry.

Why it matters: benchmark the harness separately from the model. For long research tasks, measure steps-to-answer and context burn, preserve what was checked and ruled out, then retry from a compact state instead of replaying the whole transcript.

Agent observability that closes the loop: instrument, investigate, fix, prove

From productionWeb

Honeycomb's practical loop starts with OpenTelemetry spans for every model call, tool call, and handoff. Three fields - gen_ai.conversation.id, gen_ai.agent.name, and gen_ai.operation.name - are enough to reconstruct a multi-agent run; versions, tokens, finish reasons, tool I/O, and eval scores make failures, cost, and regressions queryable.

Why it matters: do this before production traffic. A readable trace is the difference between debugging one weird transcript and finding a repeated failure pattern, tying it to a release, and proving the fix helped without moving cost or quality the wrong way.

Koa: Salesforce and NVIDIA open a CRM reasoning model with 3B active parameters

ModelsOpen modelsWeb

Salesforce post-trained NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Super on synthetic CRM workflows and says the resulting MoE matches or beats leading models on its CRM benchmark with three times fewer errors. Koa activates only 3B parameters per request, supports reasoning-budget control, and will ship on Hugging Face under an open license.

Why it matters: this is the domain-model playbook in a clean form: start from an open base, train on realistic tool trajectories, tune the reasoning budget to the task, and keep deployment inside your own trust boundary.

Grab standardized 500 agent services on one production wrapper

From productionAgentsWeb

Grab's internal LLM-Kit gives services an agent loop with evaluation, tracing, secret handling, model access, and tool discovery already wired in. More than 500 internal services use it across 50+ MCP servers; bringing a new agent service to production fell from two weeks or more to about an hour.

Why it matters: the reusable product is not the loop - it is the boring shell around it. Centralize secrets, tracing, eval hooks, model gateways, and tool registration so teams spend their time on task logic instead of rebuilding production plumbing.

ShadowPEFT turns a tiny persistent model into the adapter

ResearchToolsWeb

Instead of injecting low-rank deltas into a frozen base model, ShadowPEFT runs a much smaller trainable model beside it and fuses persistent task state into each base-model block. It is now in Hugging Face PEFT; on Llama 3.2 3B / GSM8K it reached 48.1% versus LoRA's 46.9%, with a 26 MB checkpoint but higher peak memory from the second forward path.

Why it matters: adapters no longer have to look like weight patches. A detachable shadow path can carry task behavior and even run alone for cheap inference, but benchmark memory and latency because the dual path is not free.

A business agent spent 5B tokens in three weeks and earned $1.54

From productionAgentsWeb

DFDX gave a long-running agent a computer, a small budget, and the goal of making money. After roughly 5B tokens and three weeks it had built infrastructure and attempted businesses, but earned only $1.54; the team's strongest lesson was that better environment feedback and clearer success signals mattered more than swapping in a stronger model.

Why it matters: autonomy without a measurable objective becomes expensive motion. Before upgrading the model, instrument the environment, shorten the feedback loop, make progress machine-checkable, and price the entire trajectory against the value produced.

Gemini 3.8 Live brings parallel thinking and asynchronous tools to voice agents

ModelsAgentsWeb

Google released fast and extended-thinking Gemini 3.8 Live models for real-time audio, vision, and tool use. Extended Thinking can reason in parallel while the conversation continues; both models can run tools asynchronously, and Google reports 68.6% on tau-Voice plus 35.1% on the banking variant.

Why it matters: voice-agent architecture no longer has to freeze the dialogue while the agent thinks or calls a tool. Separate the conversational fast path from background reasoning, make tool completion interruptible, and evaluate task completion alongside naturalness and latency.

Your agent passed once. IBM's Pass^k asks whether it will pass again

ResearchCraftWeb

On AppWorld, a GPT-4.1 ReAct agent averaged 77.4% across five runs but passed all five runs on only 53% of tasks. IBM's Consistency Analyzer resamples each recorded decision point without replaying tools or needing ground truth; guidelines from the unstable steps raised Pass^5 to 69% while average accuracy also improved.

Why it matters: report Pass^k beside Mean@k. An agent can be capable and still unreliable, even at temperature zero; use trace-level resampling to find flat decisions before a demo or production workflow finds them for you.

Reef turns live agent traffic into controlled model and harness updates

ToolsOpen sourceWeb

Reef is an open-source inference layer that stores traces and feedback as a structured experience stream, then updates model weights and harness components such as prompts, memory, skills, and routing. Candidate artifacts are evaluated and versioned before promotion; approved weights hot-swap into serving, while compare-and-swap prevents stale releases from overwriting newer ones.

Why it matters: learning from production needs release engineering, not an unguarded self-edit loop. Keep immutable experience records, train asynchronously, evaluate candidates off the serving path, and make every accepted model or harness change auditable and reversible.

Pay for the frontier by workload, not by default

IndustryCraftWeb

Mozilla's latest analysis puts the best open-weight models about 4.4 months behind the closed frontier. On a neutral Terminal-Bench harness, GLM 5.2 landed within a point of Claude Opus 4.7/4.8 at about one-fifth the cost per completed task; the paid frontier's clearest edge was on roughly 8-12-hour expert tasks.

Why it matters: route by verified task horizon and cost per completed task, not brand. Default routine work to the cheaper open tier, escalate the few tasks that cross its reliability boundary, and benchmark every model under the same harness.
