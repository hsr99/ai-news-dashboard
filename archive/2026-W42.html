vLLM's Oct 16 post describes an NVIDIA-built, agent-assisted CuTe DSL decode path for Kimi K3 on GB300. It reports 842 tokens/s at batch 1 on 16 GPUs in the controlled DSpark AL=6 setup, 1.73x stock vLLM there, and 1.31-1.71x without speculation across tested TP8/TP16 batch ranges.

Why it matters: The process is as useful as the number: isolate the optimization, preserve existing features, validate correctness, publish the hardware and benchmark matrix, then have humans review the evidence. The 842 tok/s headline excludes prefill and uses synthetic acceptance length 6 on 16 GB300s, so treat it as a scoped decode result rather than a serving-cost promise.

AWS shows three small MoE kernel fixes that move production latency

CraftFrom productionWeb

AWS's SageMaker post breaks down three fused-MoE serving fixes: Swap-AB improves Hopper WGMMA utilization for a 2-4% end-to-end throughput gain; routing FP8 tensor-quantized MoE work to Triton in the LMI v28 container adds 10%; and a do_not_specialize Triton annotation removes recompiles for 9% faster TTFT at TP8.

Why it matters: Profile the serving path before changing the model: kernel shape, backend routing and compiler specialization can all be production levers. These are AWS H200/LMI numbers for representative MoE workloads, so reproduce them against your own model, batch distribution, quantization and hardware before promising the same gain.
